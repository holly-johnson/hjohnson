Practice · 2017–2023

Nebraska.edu

Many sites, one shared foundation.

Departments and institutes across the University of Nebraska System each needed a website. I led the practice that built them on NUcleus for Sites in Sitecore, from kickoff and information architecture through build and training. Their own marketing staff ran the finished sites.

Client
Departments and institutes of the University of Nebraska System
Role
Led the work end to end across information architecture, UX and visual design, front-end development, Sitecore builds and client training
Users
Marketing staff who ran each site, and the audiences each site served
Built with
NUcleus for Sites in Sitecore CMS
Outcome
9 approved university brands and 20+ implementations, every site still on NUcleus
01

Every Site Built Differently

Each department and institute had its own website. Every one of them looked completely different. Brand recognition and effective communication benefit every part of the University. None of these sites spoke in a single University voice.

The team also maintained and updated the code for every site. Each one was its own codebase. Keeping all of them current became fiercely cumbersome.

02

A Repeatable Engagement

As lead designer, I developed the creative strategy from business needs, project objectives, requirements, tone and communication strategy. NUcleus for Sites kept each client’s brand consistent across projects. The design and review process ran from research, strategy and ideation through information architecture and wireframes to the finished product. The role also stretched to project manager, content strategist and building pages in Sitecore CMS when needed.

The project kickoff gathered stakeholders, project managers, content creators, designers and developers so everyone started on the same page. Specific questions asked in that meeting fed the creative brief. The project timeline started there, along with an information architecture to review with the client.

SEO practices came up early, while the client’s content was sorted into sections and pages to form the site structure. Each project also had a OneNote with sections for creative, timeline, page overviews, client action items and project processes. Clients completed the page information, such as goals and actions. Paired with the creative brief, that gave the team a way to measure the website’s success.

03

Content Before Components

In the page overview, the client set the content hierarchy for each page. That information and earlier research became a content map for every page. The client could focus on messaging and broad ideas, while I chose the best NUcleus components to carry them.

Wireframes followed from the content maps, based on strategy and the components NUcleus had available. When a new component was needed, it entered the new component design process.

When the strategy needed a component NUcleus did not have yet, I ideated, designed and developed it. That work happened directly with the brand owner, other designers and engineers.

04

Built to Hand Off

Next came the build in Sitecore, including asset creation and optimization, which I led. Content creators, other designers and stakeholders reviewed and measured the build against the content map, page goals and actions.

Client training came next, alongside an engineer. It covered using NUcleus within Sitecore, general web design best practices and tips for optimal SEO. Design consultation continued after launch as needed. That included brainstorming, problem solving, sketching and more training on NUcleus components.

05

Outcomes & Tradeoffs

NUcleus for Sites carried 9 approved university brands across more than 20 implementations. New sites launched faster, because each one started from components that already existed.

Requests to the team dropped. Clients had far more components to work with. Whenever a component was added for one brand, every other site got it for free.

The team stopped jumping from codebase to codebase. That time went into understanding how the sites should work from a University standpoint. Each brand then gave that structure its own flavor.

That time also went into improving the accessibility and usability of the CMS itself. The work included trainings and custom templates for imagery.

Tradeoffs

→Handoff vs. drift. Sites drifted after handoff. Monthly get-togethers with each department’s marketing staff helped. Where a department had no marketing staff, administrative staff came instead.

→Launch vs. upkeep. The sites launched beautiful. Quarterly meetings with the same staff reviewed new components and how they could help.

→Room to grow. The next step I wanted was more training for that staff. It would cover web design best practices. It would also show how others across the University used the same components to convey their messages.
