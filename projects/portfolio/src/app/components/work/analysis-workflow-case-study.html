Investigative Workflow Research

Connecting the investigative journey from first contact through conviction.

01. A Fragmented Investigative Journey

Investigative workflows were clunky and separated. Users moved between different tools, data types, and product experiences as an investigation progressed, carrying context across those boundaries themselves.

As the product suite expanded, the opportunity was larger than improving an individual screen or feature. The team wanted to understand and support the full investigative journey—from first contact and initial information gathering through analysis, evidence development, and the work required to help secure a conviction.

The research mapped that broader workflow so product teams could see where each tool participated, where users encountered gaps, and how separate capabilities could contribute to a more connected experience.

The investigation was one continuous journey, even when the products supporting it were not.

02. Mapping the End-to-End Workflow

Contextual interviews and workflow mapping looked beyond individual screens to understand the work surrounding them: how an inquiry begins, how information is collected and examined, and how findings move toward reporting and legal use.

The resulting model connected six parts of the workflow—query, collect, analyze, map, visualize, and report—with the decisions, context, and handoffs users navigate between them.

This was not intended as a rigid sequence. Investigations loop, branch, and return to earlier questions as new information appears. The model gave designers a shared view of the whole journey while preserving that complexity.

04. From Research to Design Decisions

The research was not treated as a separate deliverable that ended when synthesis was complete. It became a reference point for design reviews, feature work, and decisions about how products could better support adjacent stages of the investigative journey.

Rather than asking only whether a screen looked consistent or met its immediate requirements, the team could ask whether it preserved analytical context, supported movement between overview and detail, and made relationships easier to examine without overstating what the data proved.

05. Research Across the Suite

This was not a standalone product or a single shipped feature. Its value was in giving teams a shared view of the complete investigative journey while individual products continued to evolve.

Once designers aligned around that foundation, consistency became easier to maintain across products. New work could begin with an understanding of what came before, what needed to happen next, and how a decision fit into the larger workflow.

Relationship to Helios

Helios was one way the research became actionable. The workflow model aligned designers around the experience; Helios helped carry recurring decisions into reusable patterns, components, documentation, and production code.
