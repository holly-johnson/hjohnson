01. A Fragmented Investigative Journey

Investigative workflows are clunky and separated. Users move between different tools, data types, and product experiences as an investigation progresses, carrying context across those boundaries themselves.

As the product suite continues to expand, the opportunity is larger than improving an individual screen or feature. The team needs to understand the full investigative journey, from first contact and initial information gathering through analysis, evidence development, reporting, and legal use.

Mapping that larger workflow creates a shared foundation for deciding what each product needs to support, where its responsibility should end, and how it should work with the rest of the suite.

The investigation is continuous, even when the products that support it are separate.

02. Mapping the End-to-End Workflow

Contextual interviews and workflow mapping looked beyond individual screens to understand the work surrounding them: how an inquiry begins, how information is collected and examined, and how findings move toward reporting and legal use.

The resulting model connects six parts of the workflow: query, collect, analyze, map, visualize, and report. Documentation runs through every stage, preserving the sources, trails, findings, and assumptions that make the work understandable.

This is not a rigid sequence. Investigations loop, branch, and return to earlier questions as new information appears. The model gives designers a shared view of the whole journey while preserving that complexity.

04. Defining Product Direction

The research is not a deliverable that ended when synthesis was complete. It continues to guide what the products need to become.

Teams can evaluate more than the requirements of an individual feature. They can decide which parts of the workflow a product should own, how far it should stretch, where another product should take over, and what needs to pass between them.

The same foundation also guides interaction decisions within each product. Designers can evaluate whether a feature preserves sources and context, supports the right ways to examine information, and keeps findings understandable throughout the investigation.

Paired with the company’s UX principles, the workflow makes evaluating designs almost straightforward. The team can measure a decision against both the needs of the investigation and the standards for how the experience should work.

05. A Shared Guide for the Work

This is not a standalone product or a single shipped feature. It is the research foundation designers return to as individual products continue to evolve.

With the team aligned around the same understanding of investigative work, consistency is easier to maintain. New decisions can account for what came before, what needs to happen next, which product is responsible, and how the choice affects the larger workflow.

Relationship to Helios

Helios is one way the research becomes actionable. The workflow gives designers a shared understanding of the experience. Helios carries recurring design decisions into reusable patterns, components, documentation, and production code.

The Lasting Value

The research gives the team a way to make decisions beyond the feature in front of them. It connects day-to-day design work to a larger product direction and helps the suite grow with clearer purpose, boundaries, and relationships.
