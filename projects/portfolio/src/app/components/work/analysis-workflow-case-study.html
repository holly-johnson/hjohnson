Investigative Analysis Workflow

Designed dashboards and exploration workflows that help analysts navigate complex datasets and generate investigative insights.

Role
UX Designer
Timeline
2022 – 2024
Disciplines
Product UX, Data Interfaces, Workflow Design
Technologies
Figma

01. The Challenge

Law enforcement analysts investigate complex cases across large volumes of interconnected data—phone records, financial transactions, social networks, and digital communications.

Existing tools presented this data in dense tables and disconnected views, requiring analysts to manually piece together relationships and patterns.

The cognitive load was high, the learning curve was steep, and critical insights could be missed in the noise.

The challenge was to design workflows that reduce friction, surface patterns more clearly, and support the investigative process from initial exploration through final reporting.

02. The Approach

I worked closely with law enforcement analysts to understand their workflows, pain points, and mental models.

Through contextual interviews and workflow mapping, I identified key moments where the interface could better support analytical thinking.

The design solution focused on three core areas: a dashboard that surfaces critical insights at a glance, an exploration interface for investigating complex data relationships, and a structured reporting workflow that captures findings in a format ready for legal review.

These workflows were designed to align with how analysts think through investigations, not just how data is structured.

04. Impact & Outcomes

The redesigned workflow reduced cognitive load by organizing complex data into clearer, more navigable structures.

Law enforcement users reported faster case analysis, improved pattern recognition, and increased confidence in investigative findings.

By aligning the interface with investigative mental models, the system supports the analytical process rather than slowing it down.

05. Reflection

Designing for investigative workflows reinforced the importance of understanding domain-specific mental models.

Generic data visualization patterns are not enough—the interface must align with how analysts think through cases.

This work strengthened my belief that effective UX for complex systems is not about adding features, but about organizing complexity to support human cognition and workflow.
