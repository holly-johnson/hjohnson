NUcleus Design System

One system, many institutional identities.

NUcleus unified websites across the University of Nebraska System while allowing departments and institutes to maintain their own identity. Serving as product owner, lead designer and developer, I built the reusable front-end foundation first, then integrated it into the NU CMS. A second implementation, NUcleus for Apps, carried the same brand into University applications.

01

The Challenge

The University of Nebraska System needed to unify its digital presence following a major rebrand while supporting a wide range of organizations with different goals and audiences.

While the rebrand established visual direction, inconsistent implementation across sites quickly created fragmentation.

Without a shared system, components and layouts were built as one-off solutions, often outsourced or custom developed, leading to increased cost, slower progress and inconsistency across the University's digital ecosystem.

The challenge was to translate the University brand into a scalable system that could support many organizations while maintaining a cohesive identity.

02

Approach

Rather than relying on rigid page templates, I focused on creating a system of flexible building blocks that could scale across the University's diverse digital ecosystem.

Organizations needed the ability to create their own websites with unique content and structure while still maintaining a clear relationship to the broader University brand.

The system introduced reusable front-end patterns implemented as CMS components, allowing teams to assemble pages using shared structures while adapting them to their needs.

As new priorities emerged across departments and institutes, new components were added to the system, benefiting all teams and allowing the platform to evolve continuously.

03

System Foundations

NUcleus established a shared visual and structural language across the University's digital ecosystem.

Core elements such as typography, color, spacing and layout patterns defined how websites should look and behave, creating a consistent baseline across all properties.

Alongside visual design, the system defined front-end implementation patterns to ensure alignment between design and development.

This foundation translated the University brand into reusable patterns that could scale across departments, institutes and programs.

04

Component System

NUcleus evolved into a modular system of reusable front-end components designed to support a wide range of content and layout needs.

Components were developed in HTML and SCSS, with Handlebars templates used to prototype layouts and interactions. These served as the reference patterns for implementation.

Developers integrated these patterns into the CMS as configurable components, enabling marketing teams to build and manage their own websites using shared modules.

As new needs emerged, components were added to the system, expanding capabilities while maintaining consistency across the platform.

05

NUcleus for Apps

Websites were only half the University's digital estate. The other half was applications: contact and event management, data access, a learning management system. In 2017 the University of Nebraska brand had never been used inside one of them.

A website component and an application component answer to different constraints. A page section is composed once and read. A datatable is operated, in long sessions, by someone whose job depends on it. So NUcleus split into two implementations of one brand. The application half was proven inside a real product first. The patterns came out of Theorem, then were generalized.

The front-end architecture had to stay implementation-agnostic. University applications were built on different stacks, so the system defined the pattern and the markup while leaving each team free to render it in their own framework.

Every component shipped with its code, its options, its accessibility requirements, implementation notes and a version history. A component that is not documented to that level is a suggestion, not a system.

06

Impact

The work began with an agency redesign that arrived as two pages of front-end code, against a requirement to run every university domain and subdomain. The alternative was to build the shared foundation first. That choice is why the system outlasted the platform it launched on.

Before NUcleus, a department had to hope central had the capacity to help or hire outside design and development expertise. After, marketing staff ran their own sites on the tools the team built. Accessibility stopped being rebuilt site by site.

Improvements propagated. Work done for one institute reached every other domain through the shared CMS, so teams received new components and fixes without requesting them. The shared collection also made the institutes visible to one another. A team could see how another had used the same components to carry a different message and borrow what worked.

Nebraska Online moved off a separate vendor onto the system. That contract covered the site build plus ongoing hosting and a maintenance retainer, so ending it removed a recurring cost rather than a one-time one. Components built for the migration went back into the collection every CMS user could pull from.

07

Implementation

The NUcleus team operated across design and development, often moving from simple wireframes directly into working front-end prototypes.

These implementations served as reference patterns and were integrated into the CMS as reusable components.

Working closely with brand leadership in the President's Office, I translated the University brand into scalable patterns that balanced flexibility for individual teams with consistency across the ecosystem.

Over time, I became a steward of the system, ensuring it evolved while maintaining alignment with the University's broader identity.
