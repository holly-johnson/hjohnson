NUcleus Design System

One system, many institutional identities.

NUcleus unified websites across the University of Nebraska System while allowing departments and institutes to maintain their own identities. Serving as product owner, lead designer and developer, I built the reusable front-end foundation first, then partnered with developers to integrate it into the NU CMS. A second implementation, NUcleus for Apps, carried the same brand into university applications.

01

The Challenge

The University of Nebraska System needed to unify its digital presence following a major rebrand while supporting organizations with different goals, audiences and identities.

The rebrand established the visual direction, but each site implemented it differently. Without a shared foundation, teams built components and layouts as one-off solutions or outsourced them. That increased costs, slowed progress and made the university’s websites less consistent.

The challenge was to translate the university brand into a flexible system that many organizations could use without making every site look the same.

02

Approach

Rather than relying on rigid page templates, I focused on flexible building blocks that could scale across the university.

Departments and institutes needed to create sites around their own content, audiences and goals while maintaining a clear relationship to the broader university brand.

I designed and built reusable front-end patterns that developers integrated into the CMS as configurable components. This gave teams shared structures they could adapt to their needs.

New priorities from departments and institutes shaped the component backlog. Each addition expanded what every team could build.

03

System Foundations

NUcleus established a shared visual and structural language across the university’s websites.

I translated the university brand into rules for typography, color, spacing and layout, creating a consistent baseline across its websites.

Alongside the visual system, I defined front-end patterns that kept design and development aligned.

Together, these foundations gave departments, institutes and programs a consistent starting point without limiting their individual identities.

04

Component System

NUcleus evolved into a modular collection of reusable front-end components that supported a wide range of content and layout needs.

I built the reference components in HTML and SCSS, using Handlebars to prototype layouts and interactions before CMS implementation.

CMS developers integrated the reference patterns as configurable components. Marketing teams could then build and manage their own sites using shared modules.

As new needs emerged, I expanded the component collection while maintaining consistency across the platform.

05

NUcleus for Apps

Websites were only half of the university’s digital presence. The rest was applications for contact and event management, data access and learning management. In 2017 the University of Nebraska brand had never been used inside one of them.

Website and application components solve different problems. A page section is composed once and read. A data table may be used for hours by someone whose job depends on it. NUcleus therefore became two implementations of one brand. The application system was proven inside a real product first. Its initial patterns came from Theorem, then were generalized for use across university applications.

The front-end architecture remained implementation agnostic because university applications used different technology stacks. NUcleus defined the patterns, markup and behavior while allowing each team to render them in its own framework.

Every component shipped with code, configuration options, accessibility requirements, implementation notes and version history. A component that is not documented to that level is a suggestion, not a system.

06

Implementation

The NUcleus team operated across design and development, often moving from simple wireframes directly into working front-end prototypes.

I built the reference implementations, then collaborated with CMS developers as they turned the patterns into configurable components.

Working closely with brand leadership in the President's Office, I translated the university brand into scalable patterns that balanced flexibility for individual teams with consistency across the university.

As NUcleus grew, I maintained its product vision while helping the team extend it without losing alignment with the university’s identity.

07

Impact

The work began with an agency redesign that delivered two pages of front-end code for a platform expected to support every university domain and subdomain. I chose to build the shared foundation first. That decision is why NUcleus outlasted the platform it originally launched on.

Before NUcleus, departments either waited for the central team to have capacity or hired outside design and development help. After adoption, marketing teams managed their own sites using the tools we built. Accessibility no longer had to be solved separately for every site.

Improvements carried across the platform. Work completed for one institute reached every other domain through the shared CMS, giving teams new components and fixes without requiring separate projects. The shared collection also made each team’s work visible to the others. One institute could see how another had used the same components for a different message and borrow what worked.

Nebraska Online moved from a separate vendor onto NUcleus. Ending that contract removed recurring hosting and maintenance costs, not just a one-time build expense. Components created for the migration returned to the shared collection for every CMS team to use.
